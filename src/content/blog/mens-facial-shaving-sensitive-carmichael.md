## What should I tell you about my shaving and products?

Be specific. “My skin is sensitive” is a start, but details help more. Tell me how often you shave and whether you use a blade or an electric razor. Tell me where it gets irritated, like the neck or along the jaw. Tell me what you put on before and after, including aftershave and your usual skincare. If you use prescription skincare or had a recent skin procedure, mention that too. Those details are how the steps get adjusted for you.

## What helps on the days between facials?

A facial is one visit. Your shave happens most days. The American Academy of Dermatology suggests wetting your skin and hair first, using a shaving cream or gel (one labeled for sensitive skin if yours is very dry or sensitive), shaving in the direction the hair grows to help prevent razor bumps and burns, and replacing blades or disposable razors after five to seven shaves.

For washing, the AAD recommends a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser without alcohol, lukewarm water, your fingertips instead of a washcloth, and no scrubbing. Small habits, done most days.

## Is a facial a treatment for razor burn or ongoing irritation?

No. My facials are cosmetic skincare, not medical treatment, and no single visit promises a particular result. If irritation keeps coming back, hurts, or comes with bumps that won’t settle, see a board-certified dermatologist.
