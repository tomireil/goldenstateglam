It depends, and it starts with telling me. Retinol and other retinoids can make skin more sensitive, so before a facial at my Fair Oaks studio, your retinoid use is part of deciding what suits your skin that day.

Say you started a retinol serum this fall. Your skin seems fine most days, but it feels a little tight or flaky after a few nights in a row. Then you look at a deep cleanse facial that includes exfoliation and wonder whether the two belong in the same week. That’s a fair question. Here’s how I’d think it through.

## Why does retinol matter before a facial?

Retinoids keep working even when you can’t see it. The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/retinoid-retinol) notes that starting too strong or too often can irritate skin, and that retinoids make skin more sensitive to the sun. That’s why its dermatologists suggest starting slowly, using retinoids at night and protecting your skin during the day.

A facial can include exfoliation. My [Signature Deep Cleanse Facial](/services/signature-deep-cleanse/) does, along with cleansing, hydration and finishing SPF, with extractions only when they’re appropriate. Exfoliation on skin that’s already reacting to a retinoid is exactly the kind of thing to talk about first.

## What should I tell you before my facial?

Name the product, not just the category. “A retinol serum, three nights a week” tells me far more than “some anti-aging stuff.” Mention prescriptions such as tretinoin or adapalene, when you last used it, and whether your skin feels tight, flaky or stings right now.

Add any sensitivities, allergies and recent procedures. My [FAQ](/faq/) asks for the same basics before any facial.

## Should I stop retinol before a facial?

I won’t give you a set number of days here. That depends on your product, your skin and the facial. If your retinoid is prescribed, don’t stop or change it without asking the person who prescribed it. If it’s over the counter, tell me about it before your appointment so we can talk it through.

## What if I take isotretinoin?

Talk to your dermatologist before booking any facial while taking isotretinoin or if you’ve taken it recently; they’re the right person to say what’s okay.

## What if cleansing isn’t my main goal right now?

Then start with your goal instead of the most thorough option. [What a hydration-focused facial includes](/blog/hydration-focused-facials/) compares the glow and hydration facials by time and price. Whatever you choose, the same rule applies: tell me about your retinoid.

## Is a facial a fix for retinoid irritation?

No. My facials are cosmetic skincare, not medical treatment, and one visit doesn’t promise a particular result. If your skin burns, stays red or doesn’t settle, see a board-certified dermatologist.
